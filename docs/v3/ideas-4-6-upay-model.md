## 2. The 12 ideas

Each is one paragraph. `TRACK:` is the official track (`CONTEXT_v3.md` §3). `LEVER:` is `upay-model.md` §4.
`MECH:` names the mechanism changed — **incentive / who-pays / workflow / marketplace / pricing**.

### 1. Willingness-to-Pay Compiler — the take-rate schedule is optimised, not posted

**TRACK:** 04 Growth & Campaign Intelligence · **LEVER:** 4 (ARPU / take-rate) · **MECH: pricing (who-pays).** **User:** upay's payments-product owner, who today must defend a single published ladder (upay agent **1.40% = Tk 14/1,000**, inclusive of VAT and taxes — https://www.tbsnews.net/companies/ucbs-upay-offers-lowest-cash-out-fee-234661; UCB ATM **0.80%**) while the market moves *down* (bKash cut to **Tk 13.95/1,000** in Jul 2026; cross-MFS interoperability added **Tk 8.50/1,000** from Nov 2025) and cannot see demand response to any price change. The job they hire it for is *choosing a segment-level fee schedule that maximises net revenue subject to retaining volume and to a fairness floor.* **Money (L4):** the line that moves is **commission income** — `Δnet = Σ_segment [ Δtake_rate_s × value_s × (1 − churn_s(Δtake_rate)) ]`; concretely `+Tk 1/1,000 on 1m cash-outs × Tk 2,132 = Tk 21.3 lakh`, and the doc's headline `+1% realised take-rate ≈ Tk 66 cr at the bKash anchor` (95.6% of net revenue is commission, FY2024 audited — so a take-rate move lands almost dollar-for-dollar). **AI:** causal elasticity estimation (a demand-curve head per segment × ticket band × corridor) that outputs the *counterfactual* volume at a price it has never charged. A rule or spreadsheet cannot do it because the whole quantity is counterfactual: there is no column for "transactions this segment would have made at Tk 15/1,000", and a levelled historical average cannot express diminishing or inelastic demand, so the optimum is invisible to it. **Data:** **simulated-with-known-elasticity** — the generator stores each segment's latent elasticity separately from observable features so the model can be scored against the answer key, calibrated to the BB Oct-2025 ticket distribution (174.56m cash-outs @ Tk 2,132); real upay data would replace latent elasticity with actual observed responses to past fee changes and add inter-FS switching. **48-hour demo:** the judge drags one segment's fee up and down the ladder, the screen recomputes revenue-vs-volume live and lands on the maximum, and a **fairness panel refuses any schedule that pushes the burden past the floor onto a protected group** — the tool shows a movement it will not make.

### 2. Wallet-Tier Router — put each customer on the tier they actually are

**TRACK:** 02 Customer Intelligence · **LEVER:** 4 (ARPU / take-rate) · **MECH: who-pays (tier assignment).** **User:** the customer who is being charged the **1.40% primary-wallet** cash-out rate while their money arrives as a salary, a disbursement, or a remittance — inflows the upay CEO said are priced "**less than Tk 14** … in cases free of cost" across upay's documented multi-wallet design (https://today.thefinancialexpress.com.bd/trade-market/upay-offers-lowest-cash-out-fees-highest-client-security-1628439154) — and upay's product owner, for whom the misassignment is both a churn risk and a margin leak. The job: *assign the net-margin-maximising tier per customer, not the default tier.* **Money (L4 + float):** `Δnet = [Δfloat × 9.33% implied yield] + [frequency_uplift × new take_rate] − fee_give_up`, and I state the sign honestly — giving up fee can be net-positive because **float was 17.4% of net revenue at a 9.33% yield** (FY2024 audited note) and because the lower tier raises volume. The formula is deliberately written so a judge can see the term that *loses.* **AI:** classify inflow **provenance** (upay already advertises salary/disbursement/remittance provenance on the wallet — https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=bd.com.upay.customer) and predict, per customer, the tier that maximises upay's *net* including float and churn probability. A rule cannot do it because tier is not a function of one field: a remittance wallet that also spends at merchants can net better in a *third* tier than either of its two obvious ones, and a spreadsheet has no joint objective. **Data:** **collectable/synthetic** multi-wallet data with provenance events and a deliberately injected customer whose best tier flips month to month; real upay data would add the true tier population and real balance-dwell curves. **48-hour demo:** the judge changes one customer's inflow mix and watches the assigned tier, the customer's fee, and upay's net margin recompute together, then sees the tool *refuse* to demote a customer whose float makes the higher-fee tier genuinely better.

### 3. Net-Rail Router — route each movement to the rail that makes money

**TRACK:** 06 Operations & Service Intelligence · **LEVER:** 4 (ARPU / take-rate) · **MECH: workflow.** **User:** upay's payments-routing/ops owner, working in a live regulatory dispute: upay **publicly disclosed it absorbs Tk 5–8 per Tk 1,000** on cross-operator Bangla QR, **IRF has been zeroed** by Bangladesh Bank, and a **1% MDR floor** applies (https://thefinancialexpress.com.bd/trade/bangla-qr-misuse-raises-concerns-over-mfs-sustainability, 15 Sep 2026), while upay's own cash-out earns **1.40%**. The job: *route each transfer/cash movement to the rail with the best net economics, not the cheapest-looking rail.* **Money (L4):** `net_rail = gross_fee − processing_cost − expected_failure_cost`; the highest-value case is avoiding the disclosed **Tk 5–8/1,000** cross-operator cost, i.e. **Tk 50–80 lakh recovered per 1m routed transactions**, against the `+Tk 1/1,000 = Tk 21.3 lakh/1m` watermark. This is the one lever where *not* doing the obvious thing is worth real money. **AI:** a constrained router (settlement latency, failure risk, float position, corridor) that minimises expected cost subject to a service-level promise. A rule cannot do it because net depends on ticket size, corridor, time-of-day and failure rates jointly — "always use own rail" loses on the cross-operator-only corridors and "always cheapest gross fee" loses when the failure cost exceeds the fee. **Data:** **public + synthetic** — the charge schedule is public (linked above), the corridor graph and failure/time patterns are simulated-with-calibration; real upay data would replace modelled success/failure rates with actual ones. **48-hour demo:** the judge picks a corridor, sees gross fee vs net for each rail, and watches the router **override a naive "always own-rail" rule on one specific ticket** because the failure probability makes it the losing choice.

### 4. Price-Churn Shield — defend margin where it is elastic, not everywhere

**TRACK:** 04 (also 02) · **LEVER:** 4 (ARPU / take-rate) · **MECH: incentive + who-pays.** **User:** the customer most likely to switch to bKash or Nagad after the Jul-2026 cut, and upay's retention owner who today refunds or discounts broadly and cannot tell whether the discount changed anything. The job: *spend retention money only where it is incremental, and refuse discounts to customers who would stay anyway* — the difference between "this customer will stay" and "this customer will stay **because of** the offer," which `CONTEXT_v3.md` §3 names as Track 04's sharp ML signal. **Money (L4):** `saved = value_at_risk × P(retain | offer) − offer_cost`, with the offer applied only above a predicted-incremental threshold; every avoided unnecessary discount is retained commission, and the doc's headline `+1% realised take-rate ≈ Tk 66 cr at the anchor` is the sizing watermark. **AI:** a two-model uplift estimator for retention plus a competitor-switch propensity head, scored on a Qini/AUUC curve next to a plain response-model AUROC so the judge sees why the popular version is wrong. A rule cannot do it: "discount everyone at risk" pays the already-loyal, and a spreadsheet cannot see the customer who would have stayed *without* the offer. **Data:** **synthetic with injected ground truth** — known true uplift per customer, plus a deliberate **negative-uplift** segment (customers a discount *annoys* into leaving) that a response model happily targets; real upay data would add observed switching and real randomisation. **48-hour demo:** the judge opens a customer the deterministic rule would over-discount and sees the tool **refuse the offer**, with the elasticity/retention curve bending on screen; then opens one where it pays and shows the incremental return.

### 5. Biller-Side Monetization Desk — free to the customer, paid by the biller

**TRACK:** 06 Operations & Service Intelligence · **LEVER:** 4 (ARPU / take-rate) · **MECH: who-pays.** **User:** upay's partnerships/finance lead facing a hard fact — upay's own COO-signed FAQ says "**All types of bill payments are free of charge**" to the customer (https://www.ucb.com.bd/reports/downloads/Upay/upay-faq.pdf), so the bill-pay line has **no customer-fee to defend** and must earn elsewhere. The job: *find the money on the biller side and in float, and rank which billers are worth investing in.* **Money (L4, re-based):** the line that moves is **commission/other income, not customer fees** — `value_biller = biller_commission + [prepayment_float × 9.33% yield] + cross_sell_margin`; the model's unit is **taka of margin per biller per month**, and the doc's `+1%` watermarks are the yardstick, not an assertion that bill pay will carry them. **AI:** rank billers by *monetizable* value (biller concentration, payment timing → float, cross-sell propensity of the customers who use that biller). A rule cannot do it because biller value is not volume: a low-volume utility with predictable prepayment float and high cross-sell can beat a high-volume biller that is pure pass-through, and a ranked spreadsheet cannot fold three heterogeneous term types into one decision. **Data:** **synthetic + public** — synthetic biller ledger using the real, named upay billers (Titas prepaid gas, traffic fines, Indian visa fee — https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=bd.com.upay.customer); real upay data would add actual biller contracts and settlement timing. **48-hour demo:** the judge picks a biller, sees the "free to the customer, positive to upay" path broken into its three components, and watches the tool rank a small-float-poor biller *below* a low-volume one — contradicting the obvious volume sort.

### 6. Realised-Take-Rate Leakage Engine — get paid what the tariff says

**TRACK:** 06 Operations & Service Intelligence · **LEVER:** 4 (ARPU / take-rate) · **MECH: workflow.** **User:** upay's revenue-assurance owner, for whom **posted take-rate ≠ realised take-rate** because of tier misapplication, un-billed charges on failed/retried transactions, and waivers applied outside policy. The job: *find and quantify the divergence between the published tariff and the money actually collected, and route each cause to the right fix.* **Money (L4):** this is the most direct L4 idea — the metric **is** the lever. `leakage = posted_take_rate − realised_take_rate`; closing it by 1% relative is `≈ Tk 66 cr at the bKash anchor` on the commission lines, and the granular unit is `+Tk 1/1,000 recovered on 1m cash-outs = Tk 21.3 lakh`. **AI:** per-transaction expected-fee prediction vs actual charge, with unsupervised clustering of the divergence into root causes (wrong tier, rail mismatch, timing). **Explicitly not Track 01:** this is *internal tariff/billing correctness*, not fraud, scam, or account takeover — there is no adversary model and no risk score, and no case is auto-approved or auto-denied; a human applies each remedy. A rule cannot do it because the anomaly is *conditional*: the same realised/tariff gap is correct for one (tier, rail) combination and a leak for another, which a static threshold cannot express. **Data:** **synthetic with injected leakage causes** plus a clean held-out set, so the judge can be shown the true causes before the model sees them; real upay data would add genuine billing anomalies (the input that makes this production-credible). **48-hour demo:** the judge injects a tariff change mid-run and watches the engine flag the newly un-billed stream, name the cause, and show the taka at stake — then shows a false positive it correctly declined.

### 7. Agent Float Futures — an agent-to-agent float market, priced by a forecast

**TRACK:** 05 Merchant & Agent Intelligence · **LEVER:** 6 (agent productivity) · **MECH: marketplace + incentive.** **User:** the Mirpur agent whose cash-out failures spike on the 5th and 27th, and the upay area officer managing ~60 agents across two upazilas — **130,000+ agents** are upay's operational backbone (https://www.ucb.com.bd/banking/retail-banking/upay-ucb-card). This builds on `ideas-1-2-3-8` #4 (upay pre-positioning float) by changing the *mechanism*: instead of upay lending, **agents with surplus float lend to agents with a predicted shortfall, and the price is the agents' own spread**, so upay pays nothing to move liquidity. The job: *move float from where it sits idle to where demand is about to arrive.* **Money (L6):** every **completed** cash-out that would have been refused is incremental — `1m extra served cash-outs × Tk 2,132 × 1.40% = Tk 2.99 cr` at upay's agent rate (`Tk 3.94 cr` at bKash 1.85%; `Tk 1.71 cr` at upay UCB ATM 0.80%), and the aggregate is captured by `+1% per-agent volume × 130k agents ≈ Tk 24 cr`. **AI:** a quantile (P90) intraday liquidity forecast per agent plus a matching/clearing engine with expiring offers. A rule cannot do it because the funding need is a **tail** quantity that changes hour to hour and by location — funding to the mean systematically underfunds exactly the busy agents who matter, and a spreadsheet has no notion of an offer expiring mid-morning. **Data:** **simulated-with-calibration** to the BB Oct-2025 cash-in/cash-out cadence, with real road distances from OpenStreetMap (https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright) rather than a synthetic grid; real upay data would replace modelled float ledgers with actual ones. **48-hour demo:** the judge triggers a shortfall and watches the forecast expose the P90 need, the market match the agent to a nearby lender at a quoted rate, and **refuse a match** when no lender's float clears the threshold — a market that can say no.

### 8. Conversion-Priced Agent Tiers — commission follows proven throughput

**TRACK:** 05 Merchant & Agent Intelligence · **LEVER:** 6 (agent productivity) · **MECH: incentive.** **User:** an agent stuck on the standard rate while a competitor offers a discretionary "**Priyo Agent**" **Tk 13.95/1,000** for high-volume agents (https://www.thedailystar.net/business/organisation-news/news/bkash-reduces-cash-out-charge-tk-1395-4222326), and upay's agent-network owner who must decide who deserves the better economics. The job: *price each agent's commission tier on demonstrated conversion and float reliability, not on a static geography band.* **Money (L6):** `Δnet = volume_gain(agent) − commission_delta`, paid only when the volume gain is real; the watermark is `+1m extra cash-outs = Tk 2.99 cr` at the upay agent rate and `+1% per-agent volume × 130k agents ≈ Tk 24 cr`, and `bKash's Tk 187,571 revenue/agent/yr` is the derived ceiling (`upay-model.md` §4). **AI:** predict each agent's marginal conversion from float reliability, queue state and demand, and set the tier where the predicted gain exceeds the commission given up. A rule cannot do it because two adjacent agents with identical location, stock and footfall can have different reliability, so the same static tier is wrong for one of them — and a spreadsheet cannot produce a marginal, agent-specific price. **Data:** **synthetic agent panel** with reliability correlated across district and tenure, plus an injected pair of look-alike agents whose correct tiers differ; real upay data would add true conversion and commission outcomes. **48-hour demo:** the judge moves one agent's reliability slider and watches the tier, the agent's earnings **and** upay's margin move — including a **downgrade** the tool is willing to issue.

### 9. Agent Coverage Optimizer — place the 130k network where demand is unattended

**TRACK:** 05 Merchant & Agent Intelligence · **LEVER:** 6 (agent productivity) · **MECH: network design / marketplace.** **User:** upay's network-expansion planner, who has **130,000+ agents across all 64 districts** (https://www.upaybd.com/) but no view of where demand is *unattended* within an upazila, and the parent's **273-upazila** agent-banking footprint (https://www.ucb.com.bd/banking/agent-banking/business-network) as the distribution layer. The job: *raise transactions per agent by placing and repositioning agents against a demand surface, rather than adding more agents.* **Money (L6):** improving coverage raises served cash-outs per existing agent — the watermark is `+1m extra cash-outs = Tk 2.99 cr` at the upay agent rate and `+1% per-agent volume × 130k agents ≈ Tk 24 cr`, with `Tk 187,571 revenue/agent/yr` as the per-agent benchmark (`upay-model.md` §4). This is explicitly **location intelligence**, named in Track 05. **AI:** a demand-surface model (footfall proxy, transaction recency, competing-agent density) crossed with a supply model, solving where to place, reposition, or densify. A rule cannot do it because "one agent per upazila" is blind to intra-upazila clustering and route friction — two candidate sites 300m apart can differ several-fold in accessible demand, which a flat per-area rule cannot see; a spreadsheet cannot optimise a coverage objective over a continuous surface. **Data:** **public + synthetic** — real road/POI geometry from OpenStreetMap (https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright) plus a synthetic demand surface with injected hotspots; real upay data would replace the demand proxy with actual transaction density. **48-hour demo:** the judge clicks a map cell, sees unattended demand, the nearest agents' effective reach, and the optimizer's relocation choice — then watches it **decline a new-agent proposal** because a reposition beats a new hire on projected transactions.

### 10. Counter Attach Engine — one visit, more products

**TRACK:** 05 (also 04) · **LEVER:** 6 (agent productivity) · **MECH: workflow + incentive.** **User:** the agent serving a walk-in customer; raising revenue per agent without adding footfall is the sharpest L6 path, and upay's agent has bill pay, Micro DPS (NRB Bank tie-up — https://www.tbsnews.net/economy/corporates/upay-nrb-bank-partner-expand-digital-financial-services-1494556), remittance cash-out, and recharge to attach. The job: *recommend the one next product per customer, at the counter, that the customer will actually take.* **Money (L6):** `revenue/agent = Σ product mix`, so each incremental attach is direct agent productivity; the watermarks are the same as idea 8/9 — `+1% per-agent volume ≈ Tk 24 cr` across 130k agents, and **bKash's Tk 187,571/agent/yr** is the derived ceiling that attach closes toward. **AI:** a next-best-action model over the customer's history, the agent's available products, and time-of-day, with **uplift** so it recommends only where the recommendation changes behaviour. A rule cannot do it because the right next product is a joint function of that customer's ledger history and what this agent can serve *right now*, and a static "always offer DPS" script over-recommends; a spreadsheet has no counterfactual. **Data:** **synthetic cross-sell** with known ground-truth uplift per (customer, product) and a decoy product every customer already holds; real upay data would add actual attach and conversion rates. **48-hour demo:** the judge plays a counter visit, sees the single recommended attach with its incremental-revenue estimate, and sees the engine **suppress an irrelevant offer** the scripted version would have shown — before and after on one screen.

### 11. Agent Rescue Fund — pre-empt agent dormancy with a funded intervention

**TRACK:** 05 Merchant & Agent Intelligence · **LEVER:** 6 (agent productivity) · **MECH: workflow + who-pays.** **User:** an agent trending toward zero transactions, and upay's network manager — a lost agent forfeits a `Tk 187,571/yr`-class revenue stream (bKash benchmark, `upay-model.md` §4). The job: *decide which fading agents to spend money on to bring back, and — the harder half — which to leave alone.* **Explicitly not a "generic churn dashboard"** (a banned shape, `CONTEXT_v3.md` §5): this ships a **funded intervention with an uplift decision** and refuses to spend where the agent would recover anyway. **Money (L6):** restoring a dormant agent returns per-agent productivity; `+1% per-agent volume × 130k agents ≈ Tk 24 cr` frames the portfolio, and the actionable unit is `taka of rescue spend per reactivated agent`. **AI:** an uplift/churn model that separates *dying* agents from agents merely on seasonal leave. A rule cannot do it because "no transactions last month" flags a canteen agent on Eid break the same as one genuinely failing; only the agent's own seasonal pattern and neighbourhood demand separate them. **Data:** **synthetic with seasonal decoys** — an injected holiday-eligible agent, a genuinely dying agent, and a self-recovering agent; real upay data would add real dormancy and intervention outcomes. **48-hour demo:** the judge sees three look-alike "zero-transaction" agents and watches the tool correctly label one as leave, fund one, and **refuse to fund** the self-recoverer — the refusals are the product.

### 12. Flow Balancer — align cash-in and cash-out so the agent's day closes

**TRACK:** 05 Merchant & Agent Intelligence · **LEVER:** 6 (agent productivity) · **MECH: incentive + workflow.** **User:** the agent whose float runs dry at 2pm because cash-out dominates, in a business where the operator pays agents on **cash-in** and recognizes the revenue **later** — the audited deferred-commission mechanism: "commission paid to agents for performing cash in transactions for which revenue will be generated in the next financial period(s)" (bKash FY2024 audited note, https://www.legacy.bracbank.com/financialstatement/bkash-limited-audited-financial-statements-december-31-2024.pdf). The job: *keep an agent's float alive through the day by balancing the two flows he actually controls.* **Money (L6):** balancing raises **completed cash-outs** — `1m extra served cash-outs × Tk 2,132 × 1.40% = Tk 2.99 cr` at the upay agent rate — and raises cash-in volume for float; `+1% per-agent volume × 130k agents ≈ Tk 24 cr` frames the portfolio. **AI:** an intraday flow forecast that recommends, per hour, a small cash-in incentive or a specific float action *before* the dry-up, and prices the incentive against the cash-outs it saves. A rule cannot do it because the imbalance is agent-specific and time-varying — "push more cash-in" is useless at 9am and too late at 2pm, and a spreadsheet cannot price a time-local incentive. **Data:** **simulated-with-calibration** to the BB cash-in/cash-out cadence, with an injected 2pm dry-up and a seasonality shift; real upay data would add real intraday flow curves. **48-hour demo:** the judge plays an agent's day hour by hour and watches the balancer intervene at 11am with a priced cash-in nudge, showing the cash-outs that would otherwise have been refused at 2pm — then switches it off to show the dry-up.
