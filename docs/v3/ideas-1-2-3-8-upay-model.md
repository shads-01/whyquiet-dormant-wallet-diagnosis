## 0. Corrections to the source model — read before quoting any taka figure

Two figures in `upay-model.md` §4 are arithmetically wrong. Both are reproduced from the repo's own
assertion script, so they are reproducible errors, not typos I introduced.

**Correction 1 — Lever 1 (cost-to-serve) is Tk 41.5 crore per 1%, not Tk 66 crore.**
`upay-model.md` §4 Lever 1 says a 1% relative cut in cost-to-serve is worth "≈ Tk 66 crore". Cost of
services is **63.18%** of revenue (Tk 41.48bn ÷ Tk 65.65bn, bKash FY2025 — https://en.bonikbarta.com/business/dt2VlJjSIXS99Mic).
1% *relative* on that is **0.632% of revenue** = Tk 414.8m = **Tk 41.5 crore**. The Tk 66 crore figure is
1% of *revenue*, a different quantity. Corrected formula:
`value = 0.00632 × X × R` where `X` = fractional relative reduction in cost-to-serve.

**Correction 2 — Lever 3 (frequency) per-1m-cash-out is Tk 3.94 crore, not Tk 1.85 crore.**
`upay-model-calc.py:42` computes `18.50 × 1e6 / 1e7`, i.e. it assumes every cash-out is exactly Tk 1,000.
The Bangladesh Bank October-2025 average cash-out ticket is **Tk 2,132**
(https://today.thefinancialexpress.com.bd/public/trade-market/mfs-transactions-maintain-rising-trend-in-oct-25-1767372858).
Corrected:
- at bKash's agent take rate of Tk 18.50/Tk 1,000 (https://www.bkash.com/en/products-services/cashout):
  `1m cash-outs × 2,132 × 0.0185 = Tk 39.4m = ` **Tk 3.94 crore** gross;
- at upay's own **UCB ATM** rate of Tk 8.00/Tk 1,000 (0.80%) — and note this is the **ATM** rate, not
  upay's customer-facing agent rate, which is **1.40% (Tk 14/1,000) inclusive of VAT and taxes**
  (https://www.tbsnews.net/companies/ucbs-upay-offers-lowest-cash-out-fee-234661):
  `1m × 2,132 × 0.0080 = Tk 17.1m = ` **Tk 1.71 crore** gross; at the agent rate,
  `1m × 2,132 × 0.0140 = Tk 29.8m = ` **Tk 2.99 crore**.

**Correction 3 (3 Oct 2026) — the 0.80% figure is upay's ATM rate, not its agent rate.** A pricing
re-check found the take-rate ladder in `upay-model.md` §2.1 listed **upay's UCB ATM** rate beside
**bKash's agent** rate with no upay *agent* row at all. The agent row has now been added (Tk 14/1,000 =
1.400%, inclusive of VAT and taxes). Practical consequence: like-for-like, upay's agent cash-out rate is
about **24% below bKash's, not 43%** — the earlier framing overstated the gap. upay's *current* agent
rate is **UNVERIFIED**: third-party sources conflict between 1.400% and a tiered 0.7%/0.5% schedule
labelled updated 18 Sep 2026 (https://newisty.com/upay-charge-calculator). Separately, upay's own FAQ
confirms **merchant payment is free to the customer**
(https://www.ucb.com.bd/reports/downloads/Upay/upay-faq.pdf), which inverts the usual assumption that
merchant QR is simply a lower-margin cash-out.

This correction matters commercially: it means upay's revenue per cash-out is *structurally lower* than
bKash's at the published price ladder, which is an argument for ideas that shift mix away from cash-out
rather than ideas that win more cash-out volume.

L1 = `0.00632 × X × R` (X = fractional relative cost-to-serve cut) → Tk 41.5 crore per 1% at anchor.

### 1. Sponsor Market — dormant wallets reactivated by a third party who benefits

**TRACK:** 02 Customer Intelligence · **LEVER:** 2 (retention) + 8 (B2B) · **MECH: who-pays.** **User:** a garment-factory HR officer in Gazipur paying 900 workers weekly, and the 3,100 of those workers whose upay wallets went dormant after payday. The job they hire the product for is *getting cash into their workers' wallets instead of onto a bus in a physical cash van* — HR's real problem is the cash-transport cost and the Friday-afternoon payroll queue, not marketing. **Money:** today upay pays to reacquire a dormant user (a discount, an SMS blast) with no guarantee the user transacts again. This idea moves the acquisition cost onto the party who captures the value: the employer funds a per-activated-wallet bounty, upay funds only the marginal cost of servicing it. Formula stays on L2 — `350k activated × Tk 1,397 = Tk 49 crore` gross, `× 0.5 = Tk 24 crore` conservative, of which upay's share is `bounty × 350k` and the remainder is recovered as payroll volume on L8. **AI:** a gradient-boosted ranker over (days since last txn, weekday-of-payroll alignment, cash-out count in the 30d before dormancy, whether the wallet received a payroll inflow in its history, employer match) that predicts *P(this wallet activates and then transacts ≥4×/month for 3 months)* — a compound target, not "will log in". A rule cannot do this because the label is only observable 90 days out, so the ranking has to be learned from historical cohorts under the same employer; the sponsor then buys the top decile with a standing bid, and the bid price is set by a second model that estimates the marginal activation elasticity of the remaining wallet. **Data:** collectable and synthetic — employer ID, wallet ID, txn timestamps, payroll-cycle calendar; inject dormancy with a realistic hazard curve and inject a deliberate confound (the employer who *also* gives free mobile top-ups, which correlates with activation but causes nothing) so the judge can see the model reject it. Real upay data would add the payroll-inflow provenance labels its app already claims to surface (https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=bd.com.upay.customer) and the actual employer roster. **Demo:** judge picks one of three employers, sees the top-100 dormant wallets ranked, sets a bounty of Tk 200/wallet, and the app prices the deal live — "this pool yields 340 expected activations at Tk 62,400, so your effective CAC is Tk 183 versus the Tk 49 you currently spend per registered customer"; then one click flips those wallets to "activated" and the frequency line re-projects.

### 2. Incremental Offer Fund — merchants co-fund only the offers that actually cause a transaction

**TRACK:** 04 Growth & Campaign Intelligence · **LEVER:** 1 (cost-to-serve) + 3 (frequency) · **MECH: who-pays + incentive.** **User:** a Dhaka FMCG distributor's sales manager and upay's own campaign budget owner. The job: stop spending discount money on customers who were going to buy anyway. **Money:** upay's commercial-expense line, not its commission line, is what gets cut — bKash's FY2024 commercial expense was Tk 3,996,902,095 (https://www.legacy.bracbank.com/financialstatement/bkash-limited-audited-financial-statements-december-31-2024.pdf), and upay's is **UNVERIFIED**. The mechanism change is that a *share of the discount moves off upay's P&L onto the merchant's*, and only for offers the model proves incremental. Formula: `saved = campaign_spend × fraction ruled non-incremental × merchant_share`, plus `incremental txns × 2,132 × take_rate` on L3 (at 1m incremental cash-outs, Tk 3.94 cr / Tk 1.71 cr). The judge-visible number is a **rupee-of-discount-per-incremental-transaction** ratio, before and after. **AI:** uplift / causal-response estimation, explicitly named in the official brief — "this customer will transact" is weaker than "this customer will transact because of the offer." Concretely: a two-model T-learner on a holdout where assignment was randomised, plus a Qini curve and an AUUC number shown next to a plain response-model AUROC so the judge sees why the popular version is wrong. A rule cannot do it because the whole question is counterfactual — no rule can see the customer who *did not* get the offer; a spreadsheet A/B test costs six weeks and cannot rank 800k customers. **Data:** synthetic with injected ground truth — I generate the data-generating process with a known true uplift for every customer, so the judge can score the model against the answer key, and I inject a negative-uplift segment (customers the offer actively deters) that a response model will happily target. Real upay data would add actual randomisation or a genuine geo-holdout. **Demo:** judge uploads or picks a campaign; the tool reports "Tk 4.2m of spend, 61% non-incremental", shows the Qini curve, names three customers who should be *suppressed*, and reallocates the freed budget into the top-quartile true uplifts — then shows the merchant-co-funding split they would have to sign.

### 3. Agent Shift Market — agents bid for demand instead of upay allocating it centrally

**TRACK:** 05 Merchant & Agent Intelligence · **LEVER:** 1 (cost-to-serve) + 3 (frequency) · **MECH: marketplace + workflow.** **User:** the upay area officer managing 60 agents across two upazilas, and the agent himself — 130,000+ of them are upay's operational backbone (https://www.ucb.com.bd/banking/retail-banking/upay-ucb-card) and bKash runs at Tk 187,571 revenue/agent/yr. The job: today upay pushes demand to agents by allocation, which produces refusals — an agent with no float says no to a customer standing there. The product is a market where the customer-side *demand* (a cash-out request, location, ticket size, time window) is posted and nearby agents with float claim it, so demand goes to whoever can actually serve it. **Money:** L1, `0.00632 × X × R`, where `X` is the relative reduction in cost-to-serve from fewer failed and re-served transactions — **and X is exactly what the prototype has to measure**, which is honest: the size is sourced (63.18% of revenue), the mechanism is UNVERIFIED, so the deliverable is a *measured* X from a time-and-motion baseline, not a claim. Secondary L3: more completed cash-outs at the same footfall. **AI:** a claim-probability model that scores each (agent, request) pair on float depth, historical decline rate, current queue, distance, and time-of-day, then solves a small assignment problem under capacity constraints; the novelty is that the "price" is not money but a *service-level promise* (claim time, settlement time), which is what agents actually optimise. A rule cannot do it because the binding constraint is the agent's float, which changes intra-day and is not visible in any static roster; a spreadsheet has no notion of a claim expiring. **Data:** synthetic agents with float ledgers, correlated decline behaviour by district and by agent tenure, and realistic geography — public POI/road data from OpenStreetMap (https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright) gives real distances rather than a grid. Real upay data would add the actual per-agent float and decline history, which is the one thing the model cannot invent. **Demo:** judge opens a Saturday-evening map of a Savar upazila, watches six requests post, and sees three agents claim within the SLA and three go unclaimed and auto-redirect to the next-nearest floated agent — then toggles "simulate float shock" (one agent's cash-in delayed) and watches the market reroute instead of the customer walking away.

### 4. Float Pre-Positioning — upay lends working float to agents who will need it tomorrow

**TRACK:** 05 Merchant & Agent Intelligence · **LEVER:** 1 (cost-to-serve) + 3 (frequency) · **MECH: incentive + workflow.** **User:** a Mirpur agent who serves a garment cluster and whose cash-out failures spike every 5th and 27th of the month. The job: he is not short of demand, he is short of cash, and today he solves that by asking a wholesaler for credit or by refusing customers. **Money:** this is the one idea that attacks L1 through the actual mechanism the model flags as the obvious target — agent commission is inside the 63.18% cost-of-services base, and the FY2024 audited note confirms the deferred-commission mechanism ("commission paid to agents for performing cash in transactions for which revenue will be generated in the next financial period(s)"), though the **commission-vs-other split inside cost of services is UNVERIFIED**. The model delivers `expected_successful_transactions`, upay pre-funds against it, and the agent's take-rate tier is *re-tiered* on demonstrated conversion — moving him toward the "Priyo Agent" economics (Tk 13.95/1,000 vs Tk 18.50/1,000, https://www.thedailystar.net/business/organisation-news/news/bkash-reduces-cash-out-charge-tk-1395-4222326), which grows L3 rather than shrinking it. L3 formula: 1m additional served cash-outs = Tk 3.94 cr at the standard rate. **AI:** quantile forecasting of each agent's *liquidity need* (P90 cash-out value over the next 72h from cash-in cadence, cluster payroll calendars, and the Eid/Ayaya cash cycle — the seasonality is injected, not assumed), producing a pre-funding amount and an automatic clawback schedule. A rule cannot do it because the pre-funding amount is a tail quantity, not a mean — funding to the mean systematically under-funds exactly the busy agents who matter, and funding the 99th percentile ties up float that earns nothing at the 9.33% implied trust yield (17.4% of net revenue, FY2024 audited note). **Data:** simulated-with-calibration — calibrate the cash-in/cash-out cadence shape to the published industry structure (174.56m cash-outs/month at Tk 2,132 avg; 142.55m cash-ins at Tk 2,898, BB Oct-2025) and inject a real festival multiplier plus a hard floor on float. Real upay data would add the agent-level ledger, which is the true calibration target and the one input that would make the pre-funding amounts real rather than plausible. **Demo:** judge picks an agent, sees P50/P90/P99 float need for the next three days, approves a pre-funding of Tk 180,000, watches the agent then accept three customers who were previously auto-declined, and then pulls the ledger at day 7 to see the clawback settle with zero manual intervention.

### 5. Payroll-as-a-Product — upay carries the employer's float and is paid in transactions

**TRACK:** 07 Open Innovation (B2B wedge into 03/05) · **LEVER:** 8 · **MECH: who-pays + workflow.** **User:** the finance manager of a Tk-crore-a-month B2B distributor — Agrani Distribution is already a strategic upay partner with an MoU to expand nationwide B2B operations (https://www.agranidistribution.com/news) — plus foodpanda riders (https://www.ucb.com.bd/news-and-events/press-release/upay-foodpanda-partnership-to-simplify-transactions-1). The job: pay 400 field staff on time without maintaining a bank payroll file, and get the money to stay in the upay wallet rather than leaving it at the agent counter the same evening. **Money:** this is the only idea that converts a *float* line into a *product*. Trust balances earned 17.4% of net revenue at a 9.33% implied yield (FY2024 audited note) and salary disbursement is ≈3.1% of MFS value (**secondary source, UNVERIFIED**, https://directory.exports.bd/remittance) — so the honest number for a judge is a **share, not a taka total**: this is the one place the float line and the commission line reinforce rather than trade off, because wallet dwell time after payday is the product. Report it as "dwell days after disbursement" and "share of payroll that never touches an agent". **AI:** a cash-concentration model that predicts, per employer, the *hour-by-hour* wallet balance trajectory across a payroll cycle and sizes the required prefunding inside upay's own trust account — a constrained scheduling problem, not a forecast that ends in a number. A rule cannot do it because the correct prefunding amount depends on employer-specific disbursement timing and worker geography simultaneously, and getting it wrong is a regulatory/treasury event, not a rounding error. **Data:** synthetic employers with payroll files, calibrated to public Bangladesh Bank disbursement structure; the trap is to make the dwell-time uplift look universal when it is really driven by whether workers live near a UCB Agent Banking branch — 273 upazilas is a real constraint (https://www.ucb.com.bd/banking/agent-banking/business-network). Real upay data would add the employer contract terms and the trust-account balance. **Demo:** judge enters a fake 400-person payroll file with a Tk 42m disbursement date; the app returns a 14-day prefunding curve, shows the residual wallet balance at risk each day, and then runs a live simulation where 40 of the workers cash out the same evening and the app shows the prefunding curve absorbing it without breaching the trust floor.

### 6. Autopay Subscriptions — bills move from a monthly act of will to a standing instruction

**TRACK:** 06 Operations & Service Intelligence · **LEVER:** 3 (frequency) · **MECH: workflow + incentive.** **User:** a Dhaka rickshaw driver paying Titas gas, a mobile recharge and a school fee across three different merchants on three different days, all from a wallet that is usually empty on the 1st. The job: make the money arrive without a monthly decision, and — the part upay cares about — replace a *reactive* cash-out with a *scheduled* one. **Money:** the mechanic is that a subscription creates a **known, dated, predictable outflow**, which is worth more than an unpredictable one of the same size because it converts a cash-out into a bill pay plus a lower-value top-up. Bill pay is reportedly free of charge to upay customers (2021 Financial Express report, https://today.thefinancialexpress.com.bd/trade-market/upay-offers-lowest-cash-out-fees-highest-client-security-1628439154), so the direct revenue is **near zero and I am not going to pretend otherwise** — the value is L3 frequency consolidation plus the float that predictability allows (predictable balances are cheaper to prefund, and float earned 17.4% of net revenue for bKash). Report as **transactions-per-active-user per month** against a sourced baseline of 678.63m industry txns/month. **AI:** a sequence model over each customer's own bill-type history predicting (a) which subscriptions they will miss next month and (b) the smallest pre-funding that will cover them, so upay can offer a "top-up Tk 500 on the 3rd" nudge sized to the actual gap rather than a round number. A rule cannot do it because the missed-bill prediction is per-customer and seasonal — utility bills, school fees, and mobile data top-ups have different periodicity and different failure modes, and a fixed "are you okay?" SMS is exactly the generic chatbot the brief bans. **Data:** synthetic billers with realistic Bangladesh-specific billing calendars (traffic fines, Indian visa fee and Titas prepaid gas are all named upay products — https://www.upaybd.com/products/make-payment), plus a household-level shortfall process so the model has something real to learn. Real upay data would add actual biller identifiers and pay dates. **Demo:** judge picks the "worst" synthetic household in the set, sees the model flag the two bills it will miss next month and *why*, watches a sized top-up nudge appear on the right date, and then scrubs the calendar forward one month to see the household survive without a cash-out.

### 7. Complaint Autopsy — complaints clustered to operational root cause, evidence assembled automatically

**TRACK:** 06 Operations & Service Intelligence · **LEVER:** 1 (cost-to-serve) · **MECH: workflow.** **User:** upay's complaint officer and the 24/7 hotline team behind 16268 (https://www.upaybd.com/), handling the same eight complaint *shapes* thousands of times a month by hand. The job: not to judge any case, but to stop a human spending 20 minutes assembling a transaction timeline that a query can return, and to see which agent or which biller or which corridor is generating the cluster. **Explicitly not Track 01:** this builds no scam, fraud, or account-takeover classifier. The AI's only job is clustering service failures by root cause; no case is auto-approved or auto-denied, and every remedy a human already had discretion to apply still needs a human. **Money:** L1, `0.00632 × X × R` → Tk 41.5 crore per 1% at the anchor. `X` here is the fraction of complaint-handling minutes eliminated, which the prototype *measures* with a synthetic time-and-motion baseline rather than asserting — a spreadsheet can count cases but cannot tell you that 71% of agent-cash-out complaints trace to one agent's float exhaustion on the 27th. **AI:** unsupervised clustering plus an evidence-pack generator that reconstructs the case timeline from the transaction ledger and hands it to the officer; the deliverable is a ranked root-cause list with volumes and a one-click export of the evidence bundle. A rule cannot do it because complaint free-text in Bangla and English across app, USSD `*268#`, hotline, and agent channels does not cluster on keywords — the same underlying failure is described five different ways. **Data:** synthetic complaint corpus generated from a seeded root-cause process, so the judge can be shown the true clusters before the model sees them, and I inject paraphrase-heavy phrasing plus a decoy cluster of topically-similar-but-unrelated complaints. Real upay data would add real complaint text — the single input that would make this credible and is also the one that needs the strictest privacy handling. **Demo:** judge types a complaint in Bangla on a phone-sized view; three seconds later the screen shows the detected root-cause cluster, its true seeded label, its volume this month, and the full evidence timeline, plus what a human officer spent 20 minutes pulling together.

### 8. Restock Credit — merchant borrowing against a demand forecast they can audit

**TRACK:** 05 Merchant & Agent Intelligence · **LEVER:** 8 + 3 · **MECH: who-pays + incentive.** **User:** a Savar canteen and bookshop operator in the Daffodil campus catchment, where upay already has payment-acceptance agreements spanning the education ecosystem (https://www.ucb.com.bd/news-and-events/press-release/upay-and-daffodil-group-sign-strategic-partnership-mou-to-advance-digital-payments) and a UCSI agreement covering campus-wide QR, tuition, scholarships and a *planned* RFID smart student ID (https://www.ucb.com.bd/news-and-events/press-release/upay-and-ucsi-university-bangladesh-sign-agreement-to-build-a-cashless-smart-campus) — note the RFID project is **planned, not live; do not claim otherwise**. The job: stock for the exam-week spike without tying up cash in inventory that does not move. **Money:** today QR acceptance earns the merchant almost nothing and gives upay almost nothing — Bangla QR is **3% of NPSB volume and 0.5% of value** even after ~1m merchants were onboarded (Bangladesh Bank 2025 payment report as cited in `upay-model.md` §4 Lever 7; **the source URL is not recorded in that file — UNVERIFIED**). So merchant volume is a *mix-shift* lever, not a 1% lever, and this idea is honest about that. The mechanism change: upay and the parent bank put a small working-capital line against the forecast, the merchant pays for it in fee, and the forecast becomes the underwriting — a merchant who cannot explain their own forecast gets no credit. **AI:** hierarchical demand forecasting per merchant-SKU-week with heavy shrinkage toward the merchant's own history, producing (a) a reorder quantity and (b) a **credible interval the merchant disputes**. A rule cannot do it because the whole product is that the merchant audits the number — and a rule either produces an explainable average (useless for a 3-day spike) or an unexplainable one (unacceptable for credit underwriting). **Data:** synthetic merchants with a known demand-generating process, injected exam-week and Ramadan seasonality, and deliberately heavy-tailed small merchants where a naive model over-orders; real upay data would add actual merchant-category sell-through, which is the calibration target. **Demo:** judge picks the campus bookshop, sees the exam-week spike forecast with its interval, disputes the number upward, watches the credit line resize to the *disputed* figure and flag the disagreement for a human underwriter, and then sees the settlement net out of the merchant's upay receipts.

### 9. Goal Autopilot — a savings goal becomes an instruction, not a suggestion

**TRACK:** 03 Customer Innovation & Financial Independence (flagship) · **LEVER:** 2 (retention) + float · **MECH: workflow + incentive.** **User:** the exact persona the brief names — a garment worker who wants to save Tk 30,000 in six months and has, to date, accumulated nothing, because Tk 30,000 was never a line item, it was an absence. The job: make the goal a standing instruction against the day money arrives, and make the trade-offs legible ("at this rate you reach the goal in seven months, not six — here is the one week you can afford to skip"). Micro DPS already exists as a product via the NRB Bank tie-up (https://www.tbsnews.net/economy/corporates/upay-nrb-bank-partner-expand-digital-financial-services-1494556) and the parent runs agent-banking deposits across 273 upazilas. **Money:** L2 — the honest claim is that a goal-linked autopilot converts a dormant-or-drifting wallet into an active one at a much higher rate than a generic reactivation campaign, so it inherits the sourced L2 arithmetic: `1% relative on active rate = +469,860 users × Tk 1,397 = Tk 66 crore`. **Guardrail, stated because the brief states it:** no hidden fees, no nudging toward unnecessary spending, and the app must be *able* to tell the user to stop contributing. A product that can only ever say "save more" fails Track 03's empowerment requirement. **AI:** a cash-flow forecaster that estimates, from the user's own inflow *provenance* — a documented upay capability (https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=bd.com.upay.customer) — when money actually lands and what the residual is, then solves for a contribution that is affordable on the user's *worst* month rather than their average month; it also produces the plain-language category explanation the brief asks for ("I don't understand these transactions"). A rule cannot do it because payroll arriving on the 28th and a remittance arriving on the 5th produce completely different affordable contributions, and the average month is precisely the number that makes savings plans fail. **Data:** synthetic wallets with heterogeneous inflow provenance (salary / government disbursement / remittance), a month-end shortfall process, and injected one-off shocks (Eid, school fees, a medical bill) so the worst-month logic is testable. Real upay data would add the real inflow-provenance labels. **Demo:** judge enters "save Tk 30,000 in six months" on a phone view, gets a plan that *explicitly fails* on the given cash profile, raises the month to seven, names the single week where the user can skip a contribution, and then scrubs a medical bill into month three and watches the plan re-solve rather than break.

### 10. Cash-Out Substitution Bounty — pay to move a customer off the highest-cost, lowest-margin product

**TRACK:** 03 / 04 · **LEVER:** 3 (frequency, mix-shifting) · **MECH: incentive + who-pays.** **User:** a cash-out-heavy customer in a union area who walks to an agent twice a week, and upay's treasury team, which books that walk at a **1.40% agent rate of which it keeps well under half** (bKash's is 1.85%). The job: buy the *behaviour change* — move the customer onto merchant QR for everyday purchases — and pay for it explicitly rather than hope a campaign works. **Money:** the substitution is *revenue-reducing at the top line* and the idea must say so. Cash-out earns upay ~1.40%; a merchant payment is **free to the customer** and earns a merchant-side MDR of at least 1% (BB floor from 1 Jul 2026) against a cost upay itself puts at **Tk 5–8 per 1,000** on cross-operator QR (https://thefinancialexpress.com.bd/trade/bangla-qr-misuse-raises-concerns-over-mfs-sustainability, 15 Sep 2026). So the bounty is priced against *retained margin*, not gross take: `bounty < (agent commission saved + own-rail MDR − lost 1.40% − processing cost)`, and the app **refuses any bounty above that ceiling**. The counterweight is that the *customer* keeps roughly **Tk 11–30 per displaced transaction** at the Tk 2,132 average cash-out ticket — a range, because upay's agent rate is tiered below the Tk 14 headline with some wallet tiers free — which is what makes a bounty ethically easy and behaviourally effective. `bounty < (cash-out retained margin × transactions displaced)` — with the retained-margin term broken out explicitly, not hidden inside a single number. **AI:** a substitution-propensity model over (cash-out frequency, distance to nearest agent, merchant-category spend already visible on the wallet, cash-in cadence) that predicts P(this customer stops ≥1 cash-out/month after the bounty) at a *specific* bounty level — a dose-response curve, not a yes/no model. A rule cannot do it because the ceiling is customer-specific (a customer who is already spending at merchants needs no bounty; one who is 4km from the nearest agent needs a bigger one), and because the naive version subsidises customers who were already migrating, paying for organic shift. **Data:** synthetic with injected substitution elasticity and a population of "already migrating" customers as the deliberate trap; the *agent-distance* feature comes from public OSM (https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright). Real upay data would add the actual inter-FS transaction visibility that makes substitution measurable at all. **Demo:** judge sees the app price a bounty of Tk 60 for one customer and *refuse* Tk 400 for another with the reason shown on screen — "this customer already pays at 3 merchants; the marginal shift is zero" — then runs the dose-response curve and picks the efficient point.

### 11. Hotline Auto-Remedy — the officer's first action is executed, not typed

**TRACK:** 06 Operations & Service Intelligence · **LEVER:** 1 (cost-to-serve) + 2 (retention) · **MECH: workflow.** **User:** the caller who rang 16268 because a payment debited and did not credit a merchant — the highest-frequency genuine service failure in the whole catalogue — and the officer who takes the call. The job: get the customer compensated *within the call*, not after a three-day ticket. **Money:** L1 for handling time (`0.00632 × X × R`, Tk 41.5 cr per 1% at the anchor, `X` measured in the prototype) **plus** L2, because a customer whose problem is fixed on first contact does not go dormant — and 35m of bKash's 82m registered base is dormant. **AI:** a retrieval + reconciliation agent that reads the case, matches it against the ledger, and — critically — *proposes* the remedy with its evidence, in a queue the officer approves in one keystroke; it does not disburse. This is the boundary the official §14 "human oversight" line requires, and stating it is also what makes the product defensible rather than reckless. A rule cannot do it because the reconciliation is genuinely multi-step (USSD and app rails can produce the same debit through different paths), and a generic chatbot is explicitly the banned-by-official product shape. **Data:** synthetic case ledger with deliberately non-obvious cause chains — a duplicate debit, a merchant-side timeout, an agent-side float failure — each of which a naive keyword matcher confuses with at least one other; real upay data would add the real case outcomes. **Demo:** judge acts as the customer and calls in; the officer's screen shows the timeline, the proposed remedy, the exact ledger lines that prove it, and one "Approve" key; then the judge picks one of the three deliberately-confusable cases and sees the system pause and escalate to a human instead of guessing.

### 12. Campus Closed Loop — students' money cannot leave campus except through upay rails

**TRACK:** 03 / 05 · **LEVER:** 8 + 3 · **MECH: marketplace + workflow.** **User:** a second-year student at a Daffodil-group campus with an RFID student ID, a canteen card, a bookshop QR terminal, a scholarship credit and a tuition bill — five flows that today have nothing to do with each other. The job: pay for lunch without thinking, and — upay's job — turn the campus from a set of *disconnected* merchants into one closed loop where the same student balance does everything. **Money:** pure L8, and I will not put a taka figure on it. The measurable is **share of on-campus spend that stays inside the upay loop** and **merchant count per campus with >30 days of activity** — the second is the real business number because a dead terminal is pure cost. The strategic reason this is worth a prototype even at a small 1% value is stated in `upay-model.md` §4 Lever 7: merchant volume is where the mix is headed and is the only lever that converts high-value cash-out volume into high-frequency low-value volume. **AI:** a merchant-liveness model over transaction recency, category mix, and terminal failure patterns that predicts which campus terminals are about to go dark, plus a next-best-action ranking that puts upay's (small) field support where it prevents a terminal loss rather than where it is easiest to visit. A rule cannot do it because liveness is not "no transactions" — a canteen that goes quiet for 11 days is dying while a bookshop that goes quiet for 11 days is just on break, and only the merchant's own seasonal pattern separates them. **Data:** synthetic campus with per-merchant category calendars (canteen term-time peaks, bookshop exam-week peaks, a hostel-month-end pattern), plus public OSM for walk distances. Real upay data would add actual campus acceptance and terminal telemetry. **Demo:** judge picks a campus, sees 40 terminals scored by liveness risk, watches one bookshop flagged as *healthy and just on break* while a canteen is flagged as dying, and then pulls the funding slider and sees which two terminals upay's field team should visit before they die.
